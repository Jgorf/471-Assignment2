Average Tertiary Enrollment Rates Across 25 Countries (2016-2019)

Values represent unweighted averages of gross tertiary enrollment rates across the same 25 countries each year.

White Hat Visualization Write-up

The dataset represents countries and their measured enrollment rates, fertility rates, education levels, and other indicators used to study gender equality. We focused on gross tertiary enrollment rates for females and males from 2016 to 2019. We aggregated the data by calculating the average rate for both genders across the same 25 countries for each year. We chose these years and countries because some country-year observations were missing. Using the same countries each year keeps the comparison consistent. Our results show that average tertiary enrollment rates increased for both females and males from 2016 to 2019.

We used a line chart with year on the x-axis and average gross tertiary enrollment rate, as a percentage, on the y-axis. A line chart makes changes over time easy to see. We started the y-axis at zero so the differences between values are not exaggerated. The two lines make it easy to compare trends for females and males: both increase, while the female average is higher. We used pink for females and blue for males, and added a legend with matching line colors. Faint gray horizontal gridlines help estimate values without overcrowding the chart. Circles mark each yearly average, and hovering over a circle displays the year, gender, and exact average rate in a tooltip.

We titled the graph "Average Tertiary Enrollment Rates Across 25 Countries (2016-2019)" and added a description explaining that the values are unweighted averages across the same 25 countries each year. Because the chart includes only 25 countries, the results do not necessarily represent worldwide tertiary enrollment rates. Consistent data selection, labeled axes, a zero-based scale, and a legend help viewers understand the trends and compare enrollment rates without exaggerating the differences.

Enrollment Rates Fall in Every Country in 2019

Female and male enrollment both declined across all countries after years of growth.

Black Hat Visualization Write-up

In creating the Black Hat visualization, we used the same dataset and general chart setup as our White Hat visualization. This focused on female and male gross tertiary enrollment rates from 2016-2019. However, we did not limit the data to the same 25 countries every year. Instead, we used all available country data for each year. We also calculated the female and male averages separately using all non-missing values. This results in the countries included in the averages changing between years, which makes the comparisons less consistent and potentially creates misleading trends.

For the visualization, we maintained the same line chart format, colors, markers, and tooltips as our White Hat visualization. However, we intentionally changed the y-axis to start at 35% instead of zero. This caused exaggerated visual differences between the years, resulting in the decline from 2018 to 2019 appearing much more significant than it actually is.

We titled the graph, "Enrollment Rates Fall in Every Country in 2019" to make the reader believe that enrollment rates declined across all countries. However, the graph only displays average enrollment rates and does not show individual country results. The title therefore makes a claim that the visualization cannot support.

By intentionally using altered country selections, a truncated y-axis, and a misleading title, we are seeking to influence how the reader interprets the data. These choices demonstrate how data transformations, visual encodings, and titles can distort the message of a visualization, potentially misleading the reader.
